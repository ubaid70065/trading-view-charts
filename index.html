TradingView Integration Playground

Four ways to put a trading chart on a page — compared side by side. All four use real market data; they differ on two axes: free vs. licensed, and TradingView's data vs. your own.

The one question that picks your path: do you need to display your own data, or just show market data? Showing market data → the free embed is enough. Your own prices/symbols/backend → you need a datafeed (Lightweight Charts or the licensed Charting Library).

📈 Advanced Chart (Embed)

TradingView-hosted widget

Free No backend TradingView data

Drop-in widget from a CDN. Real stocks, forex, crypto, indices out of the box with symbol search. Zero setup — but you can't feed it your own data or deeply customize it.

🌐 Charting Library · UDF

Licensed widget + HTTP backend

Licensed Your data HTTP + WS streaming

The built-in UDF adapter talking to a Node backend (udf-server.js) that serves /config, /symbols, /search, /history, /quotes from Binance — plus a wrapper that adds real-time WebSocket streaming the UDF adapter lacks.

Which should you pick?

  • Just embedding charts on a website → Advanced Chart embed. Nothing else comes close on effort.
  • Your own data, free, full control of look → Lightweight Charts. Open-source, no license, you build the UI.
  • Your own data + TradingView's full pro UI (drawing tools, indicators, replay) → licensed Charting Library. Datafeed API for flexibility/native streaming; UDF for the fastest backend to stand up.
